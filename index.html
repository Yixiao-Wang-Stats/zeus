Meet ZEUS

Denoising generative models deliver high-fidelity generation but remain bottlenecked by inference latency due to the many iterative denoiser calls required during sampling. Training-free acceleration methods reduce latency by either sparsifying the model architecture or shortening the sampling trajectory. Current training-free acceleration methods are more complex than necessary: higher-order predictors amplify error under aggressive speedups, and architectural modifications hinder deployment.

Beyond 2x acceleration, step skipping creates structural scarcity—at most one fresh evaluation per local window—leaving the computed output and its backward difference as the only causally grounded information. Based on this, we propose ZEUS, an acceleration method that predicts reduced denoiser evaluations using a second-order predictor, and stabilizes aggressive consecutive skipping with an interleaved scheme that avoids back-to-back extrapolations.

ZEUS adds essentially zero overhead, no feature caches, and no architectural modifications, and it is compatible with different backbones, prediction objectives, and solver choices. Across image and video generation, ZEUS consistently improves the speed–fidelity performance over recent training-free baselines, achieving up to 3.2x end-to-end speedup while maintaining perceptual quality. The code is publicly available at github.com/Ting-Justin-Jiang/ZEUS.

A Tale: Lightning of ZEUS

At step $t$, the denoiser has computed $\psi_t$. The backward difference $\Delta^{(1)}\psi_t = \psi_t - \hat{\psi}_{t+1}$ encodes information that $\psi_t$ alone cannot provide—the response of the denoiser to the given (approximated) state. Together, the pair constitutes the observed information set, available at zero marginal cost.

Given the observed information set, we can add the elements together, which forms a second-order predictor:

$\hat{\psi}_{t-1} = \psi_t + \Delta^{(1)}\psi_t = 2\psi_t - \hat{\psi}_{t+1}.$

Rather than computing new approximations, we simply cycle between the full evaluation and its second-order extrapolation. This interleaved pattern applies the second-order predictor once (at $j=1$), then resets to $\psi_t$, then reuses the same pair—never chaining predictions.

How ZEUS Fly

ZEUS uses a second-order numerical predictor that extrapolates the next denoiser output from the observed information set at the most recent full-evaluation step. Its interleaved scheme reuses the same pair across consecutive reduced steps, as shown in Figure 3.

Evaluations

All experiments use official checkpoints, HuggingFace Diffusers, FP16 precision, and 50 sampling steps unless otherwise noted. All runs are performed on a single NVIDIA A100-80GB GPU. We report end-to-end wall-clock speed up.

Quantitative Results

Image evaluation uses 5,000 MSCOCO-2017 validation prompts; video evaluation uses Penguin Benchmark prompts optimized as provided by the VBench team. Video baseline numbers follow the reported SVG-series setup. Similarity metrics compare accelerated samples with full evaluation; video metrics are frame-averaged.

ZEUS approximates the middle 70% of the trajectory, keeping the first 20% and last 10% as full evaluations. The main image presets Medium / Fast / Turbo use r = 2 / 3 / 4 consecutive reduced steps. Swipe within a table on small screens to see every metric.
